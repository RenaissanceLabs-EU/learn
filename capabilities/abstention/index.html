Knowing what it doesn't know

The model says "I don't know" when it does not know, and answers when it does.

1In practice

Who is Declan Monmonley? (a made-up name)

Without it

"Declan Monmonley is a British actor, writer, and director..."

With it

"I'm not sure who Declan Monmonley is. Could you provide some context?"

Design and pre-training

Nothing in pre-training separates "I know this" from "this sounds right".

Post-training and deployment

Supervised fine-tuning usually makes it worse, because every training question gets a confident answer. It has to be trained on purpose.

3How to measure it

  • Natural-question hallucination benchmark500 questions in 10 languages, with made-up subjects and real people, and no offered way out.
  • Release gates used with itFabrication under 10%. Refusals on well-known people under 5%. At least 95% of answers in the right language.
  • SimpleQAShort factual questions, graded as correct, incorrect or not attempted.

6What it costs

  • The tuned model declines 31 of 80 questions about well-known people. It had answered 20 of those wrongly before, and 3 fully correctly.
  • It tells users it is not sure who some famous people are, such as John Paul II.
  • Thinking mode, which was not trained, lost accuracy on known facts.

7Findings

  • An offered "UNKNOWN" option does not transfer to natural questions.OpenEuroLLM
  • DPO alone raised reward accuracy to 79% and barely changed what the model wrote.OpenEuroLLM
  • Twelve minutes of supervised fine-tuning took fabrication from 97.7% to 3.6%.OpenEuroLLM
  • Most of the measured over-refusal replaced answers that were wrong.OpenEuroLLM
  • Benchmarks that give no penalty for a wrong answer reward guessing.Kalai and colleagues
