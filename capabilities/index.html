Capabilities

What goes into a model

A language model is a bundle of separate abilities. Some are learned from raw text in pre-training. Others are taught afterwards, one at a time. For each one: how it is measured, what data it needs, which recipes improve it, and what is still missing.

Learned from text

What pre-training gives you

These come from reading a very large amount of text. Later stages can sharpen them, and they cannot add what the text never held.

Taught afterwards

What post-training adds

These do not appear on their own. Each one is taught on purpose, with its own data and its own test.

Following instructionsThe model does what was asked, in the format asked for, across a conversation.1 recipe · 1 finding · 1 wantedKnowing what it doesn't knowThe model says "I don't know" when it does not know, and answers when it does.1 recipe · 5 findings · 4 wantedKnowing who it isThe model states its own name, maker, knowledge cutoff and limits correctly.1 recipe · 3 findings · 1 wantedBehaving as intendedThe model is helpful and honest, declines what it should, and respects limits on what it may do.1 recipe · 3 findings · 2 wantedUsing toolsThe model calls tools correctly, reads the results and finishes a multi-step task.3 findings · 2 wantedCalibrated decisionsThe model answers a typed question with a probability that matches how often it is right.2 findings · 1 wanted

Wanted

What nobody has published yet

Open requests, one capability at a time. If your lab has one of these, a recipe, a dataset or a short finding answers it.

  • Fluent textHeld-out loss per language for the smaller European languages, published next to the training mix.
  • World knowledgeA way to tell, per question, whether a model knows the answer or is guessing, without sampling it eight times.
  • Many languagesNative-speaker review of machine-translated evaluation sets.
  • Many languagesPreference data written in more than 12 languages.
  • Long contextA long-context test in European languages other than English.
  • CodeA recipe card for a software-engineering fine-tune on an open 8 to 9B model.
  • ReasoningPreference pairs in thinking mode for the hallucination recipe.
  • Following instructionsAn instruction-following test with programmatic checks in more European languages.
  • Knowing what it doesn't knowAnswer rows for open questions about well-known people.
  • Knowing what it doesn't knowA fairer over-refusal gate, counting only questions the untrained model got right.
  • Knowing what it doesn't knowGeneral evaluations (maths, code, tools) after this training.
  • Knowing what it doesn't knowThe same recipe on a model outside this family.
  • Knowing who it isResults on the held-out set after each later training stage.
  • Behaving as intendedIndependently reviewed full-suite runs of the benchmark.
  • Behaving as intendedScenarios written by native speakers in the other 35 languages.
  • Using toolsOpen tool-use environments in European languages.
  • Using toolsA recipe card for a tool-use fine-tune.
  • Calibrated decisionsDecision data from real case files, not generated from rules.
