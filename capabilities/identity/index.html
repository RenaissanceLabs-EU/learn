Knowing who it is

The model states its own name, maker, knowledge cutoff and limits correctly.

1Where it comes from

Pre-trainingNot shaped here
Supervised fine-tuningMostly shaped here
Preference tuningShaped a little
Reinforcement learningNot shaped here
Pre-training

A base model has read about other models, so asked who it is, it often names one of them.

Post-training

A small identity dataset mixed into supervised fine-tuning teaches a fixed set of true statements about the model.

2How to measure it

  • Held-out identity set with gatesCorrect at least 90%, wrong at most 3%, worst category at least 75%, right language at least 95%, overtrigger at most 4%.

3Common datasets

  • OpenEuroLLM Model Identity5,508 training and 923 validation conversations in 36 languages.

4Recipes

Trained, not measuredSFT warm start, then on-policy DPO, against hallucinationIdentity data was in the mix. Not measured afterwards.OpenEuroLLM · Run once by its author. Not yet reproduced by anyone else.

5What it costs

  • A model can overtrigger and talk about itself when nobody asked. The gates include a limit for that.

6Findings

  • Before identity training, the model was correct about itself 52.8% of the time and wrong 38.6%.OpenEuroLLM
  • About 0.5% of the fine-tuning mix is enough, mixed in once without upsampling.OpenEuroLLM
  • Identity is a property of the whole data mix. Rows elsewhere that claim another name can outvote the identity set.OpenEuroLLM
