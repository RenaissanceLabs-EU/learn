Part 2

Feeding the model

Data, scale and the memory a model needs.

05Pretraining dataFrontier models read tens of trillions of tokens, but most of the raw web is thrown away first. Quality, mixture and repetition shape what the model learns.Intermediate · 9 min✓06Scaling laws: how big, how longLoss falls smoothly as parameters, data and compute grow. Scaling laws turn that into a budgeting tool, and also show where the tool stops working.Intermediate · 9 min✓07Parameters, precision and memoryA parameter is a stored number, and every number costs bytes. Precision, gradients and optimizer state decide whether a model fits on one chip or needs a cluster.Intermediate · 8 min✓

Part 3

The machine room

Chips, parallelism and what goes wrong at scale.

08GPUs and parallelismA GPU is fast only when its arithmetic units stay fed. Training a large model means splitting it across thousands of GPUs in several ways at once, and paying for the communication.Advanced · 10 min✓09Mixture of expertsA mixture-of-experts model stores many specialist sub-networks but sends each token to only a few. It gets the capacity of a huge model at the arithmetic cost of a much smaller one.Intermediate · 8 min✓10When training breaksAt frontier scale something is always failing. How labs survive broken chips, loss spikes, silent wrong answers and fragile low-precision numbers.Advanced · 8 min✓

After pre-training

Turning a text predictor into something useful, and running it.

What each one keeps, and what it costs.Intermediate · 8 min✓15Inference and samplingHow a trained model produces text: a fast reading phase, a slow one-token-at-a-time writing phase, a memory called the KV cache, and the sampling rules that pick each token.Intermediate · 9 min✓16What "open" means for AI models"Open" can mean downloadable weights, public code, released data or a full training recipe. These are different things, with different freedoms, and few releases include all four.Beginner · 8 min✓
