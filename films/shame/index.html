The other side of embarrassment

What shame is and how to move through it

11 minBeginner12 chapters

Embarrassment is part of our need to belong, but its alarm often overestimates how closely others watch and how harshly they judge. The film separates embarrassment, guilt and shame, shows what avoidance costs, and offers a short self-compassion practice and a week of small, safe steps.

What to take away

  • Embarrassment works as a kind of nonverbal apology, a sign that you care about the relationship.
  • Guilt says I did something wrong, while shame says I am something wrong, and shame tends to push toward hiding.
  • The spotlight effect means we overestimate how many people notice our mistakes.
  • People often think better of us than we expect, and are more willing to help than we predict.
  • Name it, remember it is human, speak to yourself like a friend, and tell one safe person.
