What it takes to train a frontier model

Data, chips, failures and cost

15 minIntermediate13 chapters

A frontier model is a file of numbers, but producing it takes tens of trillions of tokens, tens of thousands of chips and a great deal of engineering. Using only 2026 reports (GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K2.5, a Meta fault-tolerance paper and Epoch AI data), the film follows data, scaling, hardware, failures, post-training and cost, and flags what is an estimate.

What to take away

  • Pre-training reads tens of trillions of tokens, and most raw web text is filtered out before it is used.
  • A common rule of thumb puts training compute at about six times the active parameters times the tokens.
  • At 100,000 GPUs a failure arrives roughly every 18 minutes, so fault tolerance matters as much as raw speed.
  • Post-training now runs on huge fleets of sandboxed environments, and reward design is part of the job.
  • None of the three reports gives GPU hours or dollar costs, so cost can only be estimated from the buildout.
