You are not alone in here

Why struggle feels private, and what research says

10 minBeginner12 chapters

Struggle feels private because we see only our own inner world and other people's outer one. The film looks at how alike our inner lives are across languages, why humans are built to connect, and the gaps in how we judge each other. It closes with a week of small reaching-out practices.

What to take away

  • Many people privately feel the same thing and assume they are alone, which psychologists call pluralistic ignorance.
  • Common humanity, remembering that struggle is part of being human, is one of the three parts of self-compassion described by Kristin Neff.
  • After conversations, people tend to underestimate how much their partner liked them, a pattern called the liking gap.
  • Reaching out, with a check-in or a thank you, tends to be appreciated more than the person sending it expects.
  • Psychology has not shown one shared mind. That question belongs to philosophy and contemplative traditions.
