Understanding your attachment style

Your own pattern, and how to help someone you love

11 minBeginner12 chapters

A practical companion to the research film on attachment. It helps you notice your own style (secure, anxious, avoidant or fearful), explains why it once made sense, and offers small practices for each. It also shows how to be a secure base for someone you love, and ends with a simple week of practice.

What to take away

  • Your reaction to closeness is a pattern you learned, and each pattern was a sensible answer to the home you grew up in.
  • Secure does not mean no fear or conflict. It means you can ask for what you need and survive hearing no.
  • Earned security describes people with hard childhoods who became secure adults, often with at least one person who stayed.
  • To be a secure base, stay when someone is upset, cheer for their life, and repair after you mess up.
  • A small weekly practice is to notice, pause, ask, repair and receive.
