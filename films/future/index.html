Minds · Memory, time and attention

The future is a story you tell today

Why we mispredict our own happiness

9 minBeginner11 chapters

Second film in a small series on time. The brain simulates the future by remixing the past, and the simulation errs in predictable ways: we overestimate how long bad things hurt and how long plans take. The film ends with practical tools, including honest planning, if-then plans and kindness to your future self.

What to take away

  • We imagine the future with the same brain systems we use for memory, so it is built from pieces of the past.
  • People tend to overestimate how long a setback will hurt, because they forget their own mental immune system.
  • Asking how long a task took last time removes much of the planning fallacy.
  • In studies of people with generalized anxiety, most worries did not come true, and when they did, people coped better than expected.
  • Picturing the obstacle alongside the wish, then making a small if-then plan, works better than fantasizing about success.
