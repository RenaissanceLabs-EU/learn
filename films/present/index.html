Minds · Memory, time and attention

The only place experience happens

The present moment, honestly

9 minBeginner12 chapters

Third film in a small series on time. It asks what the present actually is: a short window your brain assembles and attention steers. You meet the gorilla nobody sees, the cold-water experiment, the mind that wanders, and an honest account of what mindfulness and awe can do.

What to take away

  • Minds wander nearly half the time, and what matters most is whether the wandering is pleasant and interesting.
  • The experienced present is assembled by the brain, and attention decides a great deal about what makes it in.
  • Memory keeps the worst moment and the ending, so the remembering self and the experiencing self disagree.
  • Mindfulness programs show small to moderate benefits, and have not been shown to beat other active treatments.
  • Awe can make time feel more available, and a minute of really looking is a cheap way into the present.
