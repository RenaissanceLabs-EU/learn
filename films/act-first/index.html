Act first, and do what you say

Small actions, self-trust and kind follow-through

8 minBeginner11 chapters

The gap between saying and doing is one of the most common human experiences, and it says nothing about your worth. The film looks at why motivation often follows action, why keeping your word is really about self-trust, and four rules: promise less, shrink the action, plan when and where, and forgive the gaps.

What to take away

  • Waiting to feel ready is a trap: in behavioral activation, people schedule small, meaningful actions before they feel like doing them.
  • Shrink the action until you are almost certain to do it, because finishing small things builds confidence.
  • An if-then plan decides the when and where in advance, so you are not negotiating with yourself in the moment.
  • The twenty-one-day habit rule is a myth: in one study, reaching a stable habit took anywhere from eighteen to 254 days.
  • After a slip, self-compassion was linked with more motivation to improve, not less.
